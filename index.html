How growth actually happens

Doing alone doesn't build skills. Learning alone doesn't either. iLitUp pairs them.

Every real-world project a student takes on is paired with a short learning module on the same skill — and the combination earns a leveled credential. That's the loop that produces real, certifiable growth.

01 Do

Take on real work.

A tutoring program. A park cleanup. A coding club. A school newspaper role. Whatever the project, iLitUp tags it to the skill domain the student is actually practicing.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What does a "skill credential" mean on iLitUp?
A skill credential is a leveled record of growth in a specific domain — communication, leadership, data literacy, programming, environmental science, and 20+ more. Levels are earned through a mix of real-world projects (verified by NGO admins) and short, focused learning modules mapped to each skill. Every credential traces back to specific evidence, so it's not just a self-claim — it's auditable.
How does iLitUp help NGOs and corporates?
NGOs and corporate CSR teams get a live impact dashboard showing exactly what their programs are growing — hours, demographics, geography, skill outcomes, and retention. Reports are grant- and ESG-ready out of the box. Corporates sponsoring skill-building programs also get a workforce-pipeline view — credentialed young people in their communities ready for internships and apprenticeships.
Is iLitUp free for students?
Yes — always. Districts and corporate sponsors fund the platform; students pay nothing. We believe credentialing real growth shouldn't be a paywalled privilege of students whose families can already afford it.
How is this different from a volunteer-hour tracker?
Hour tracking is table stakes — and it doesn't tell you anything about what a student actually grew into. iLitUp is built around skills: which ones a student is building, how those skills level up over time, and which careers and college paths those credentials unlock. Hours show up under the hood as evidence; skills are what students, counselors, funders, and employers actually care about.
Who can become a partner?
We're actively building partnerships across three groups: learning platforms whose courses can be mapped to our skill framework, NGOs who run real-world programs young people grow through, and districts and CSR teams who want to fund and measure the skills high schoolers are building. If your organization fits any of those — get in touch through the waitlist with "Partner / org" selected and we'll set up a conversation.
What does sponsorship look like for corporates?
Corporates can sponsor branded skill-building programs (e.g., "Microsoft Coding Volunteers", "Walmart Day of Service"), fund scholarships routed through iLitUp to high-performing students, or subscribe to aggregated impact dashboards for ESG and CSR reporting. Sponsorships range from $5K–$50K per program, with enterprise pipeline access available for larger commitments.
How is student data handled?
iLitUp is built to align with FERPA. Student records belong to the student and their district — never sold, never shared with third parties without explicit consent. Aggregated dashboards seen by NGOs and corporates use de-identified data, audited before any reporting use.
Where are you available?
We're piloting in New Jersey and New York City first, with select partner NGOs and a small number of districts already onboard. We're currently focused on high school students, with extension to college students planned for later phases. Year 2 expands into the broader tri-state area; nationwide expansion follows district and corporate demand. Want us in your community? Get on the waitlist with your organization name.
